## Water the crop can use
The total available water (TAW) in the root zone is the water between field capacity and wilting point over the depth of the roots: TAW = 1000 x (field capacity - wilting point) x root depth, with the soil values as fractions by volume and the root depth in metres, giving millimetres of water.
The readily available water (RAW) is the part the crop can take without stress: RAW = p x TAW. The fraction p depends on the crop and is given for a crop water use of about 5 mm per day; it is lowered when the weather is hotter and demand is higher, using p = p_table + 0.04 x (5 - ETc), and kept between 0.1 and 0.8.

## Depletion and when to irrigate
Root-zone depletion (Dr) is how much water is missing compared with field capacity. From a measured soil moisture it is Dr = 1000 x (field capacity - measured moisture) x root depth. Irrigation is due when Dr reaches RAW, and the net depth to apply should not be larger than Dr, so that water does not drain below the roots.

## Rain
Rain adds to the soil water. Very small daily rain, below about 0.2 times the reference evapotranspiration, evaporates and is ignored. Rain larger than the room left in the soil (more than Dr) drains below the root zone, which can carry nutrients away with it (deep percolation).
